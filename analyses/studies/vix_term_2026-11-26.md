# VIX term structure study, 26 Nov 2026

Question: when the VIX curve flips into backwardation (near-term fear priced above longer-term), do S&P moves get bigger, or do the desk's ranges miss more, beyond what VIX level and recent realized vol already tell us?

Data: Cboe daily closes of VIX9D, VIX, VIX3M and SPX, 4 Jan 2011 (first VIX9D) to 18 Nov 2026: 3,993 days with a full week of outcomes after them. Backwardation (VIX ≥ VIX3M) on 304 of them, in 60 separate episodes. All free, from cdn.cboe.com.
Script: `analyses/studies/vix_term.py`. Output: `analyses/output/vix_term_latest.json`. Tracker: `analyses/trackers/vixterm.py`.

## Short answer

Yes, it carries real information, but about the **coming week**, not tomorrow, and the desk's Rule 5 range already uses most of it.

1. **Next 5 days' realized vol: the curve helps, out of sample.** Fit on 2011–2018, scored on 2019 onward (1,982 days). Predicting next-week S&P realized vol from VIX level plus 20-day realized vol explains 42% of the variation (R²). Adding VIX/VIX3M and VIX9D/VIX lifts it to 52%. Against a stronger baseline (adding 5-day realized vol, yesterday's move and the 5-day VIX change) it still goes from 44% to 52%. A 21-day block bootstrap puts the error improvement's 95% interval clear of zero in both cases.
2. **Next-day move size: small, not proven.** Against the stronger baseline the gain crosses zero (95% interval −0.004 to +0.031). Against the plain baseline it is barely positive. Don't lean on it for one-day calls.
3. **Range misses.** A plain VIX-implied 80% range missed 21% of backwardation days vs 5–11% in contango, so VIX underprices those days. The desk's live Rule 5 range already switches to a VIX floor in backwardation and missed 20.1% there, right on target. Its weak spots are elsewhere: **flat curve** days (VIX/VIX3M 0.95–1.0) missed 25% overall and **hot front end** days (VIX9D/VIX ≥ 1.05 while VIX/VIX3M is still in contango) missed 28%, vs the 20% target.

## Holding VIX level fixed

Backwardation tends to happen when VIX is high, so the fair test compares days with similar VIX.

| VIX | Backwardation days (episodes) | Move vs VIX-priced, backwardation | Same, no backwardation | VIX-range miss, back / not |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 15–20 | 36 (12) | 0.89 | 0.60 | 19% / 11% |
| 20–25 | 87 (13) | 0.86 | 0.60 | 24% / 9% |
| 25–35 | 97 (12) | 0.80 | 0.69 | 19% / 12% |
| 35+ | 84 (1) | 0.95 | 0.55 (n=9) | 23% / 11% |

At the same VIX, backwardation days moved about 1.2 to 1.5 times as much relative to what VIX priced. The 35+ row is essentially one episode (March 2020) plus a handful, so ignore it. Splitting the sample in half, backwardation days moved more than VIX priced in both 2011–18 (0.83 vs 0.56 on other days) and 2019 on (0.93 vs 0.58); that split is not VIX-matched.

| Steep contango (<0.85) | 1,195 | 0.43% | 0.46 | 16% | 0.62 |

| Backwardation (≥1.00) | 304 | 1.73% | 0.87 | 20% | 0.99 |

Read: in calm contango, VIX overprices realized moves by a lot (the usual variance premium). In backwardation it roughly prices them fairly.

## Direction, for the record only

After backwardation days, the S&P was up over the next 21 sessions 72% of the time (avg +2.9%) vs 67% (+0.9%) on all days. Overlapping windows and 60 episodes; not tested for significance. Not a signal.

## What changes and what doesn't

- New context line on the desk: curve state, ratios, percentile since 2011, sessions in state, and an expected next-week realized vol from the out-of-sample-tested model (with and without the curve). It never moves a probability.
- **Candidate for the Sunday review, not adopted:** widening Rule 5 on flat-curve and hot-front days too. A quick check (storm if VIX/VIX3M ≥ 0.95 or VIX9D/VIX ≥ 1.05) cut those days' misses to 9–18% but pushed overall misses under the 20% target (17.7% from 2019 on, ranges about 3% wider). It needs a gentler version and a proper out-of-sample fit before it touches Rule 5.
- **Candidate:** use the curve model for any week-ahead vol or range call. The evidence is good on 2019-on data; still, adopting it is a method/notes change.
