# Streak and reversal study, 26 Nov 2026

Question: after a big S&P day or a run of down days, does the next day tend to follow or reverse?
Data: 267 daily S&P 500 moves, 3 Nov 2025 to 25 Nov 2026 (analyses/data/spx_daily.csv plus Desk Daily closes).
Script: `analyses/studies/reversal.py`. Output: `analyses/output/reversal_latest.json`.

| After... | n | Next day up | 95% range | Avg next move |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Any day | 266 | 51% | 45–57% | +0.03% |
| An up day | 136 | 55% | 47–63% | +0.06% |
| A down day | 130 | 47% | 39–56% | −0.01% |
| A drop of 1%+ | 33 | 58% | 41–73% | +0.28% |
| A drop of 2%+ | 5 | 80% | 38–96% | +0.56% (n too small) |
| A gain of 1%+ | 29 | 55% | 38–72% | +0.06% |
| 2 down days in a row | 68 | 53% | 41–64% | +0.09% |
| 3 down days in a row | 32 | 66% | 48–80% | +0.31% |

What it says, in plain words:
- On ordinary days there is a faint "follow yesterday" tilt: the next day went the same way 144 times out of 266 (54%). Every range crosses 50%, so this is not a proven edge.
- After big drops, and after three down days in a row, the next day leaned the OTHER way (a bounce), the opposite of "follow yesterday". The most recent example: −3.7% on 20 Nov, then +4.7% on 23 Nov.
- So the follow-yesterday baseline beat the desk in November because November kept falling, not because the rule works in general. If the desk adopted it, it would be fitting one month.

What it does not do: change any probability. method/notes says new signals stay context-only until tested on more data. One year of daily data is small. Re-run it once the data backbone's longer FRED history lands (analyses/data/history/SP500.csv), which would give decades instead of one year.

Not investment advice.
