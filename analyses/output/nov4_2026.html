Market What-Ifs: 4 Nov 2026

Agent-based simulations (marketsim) of three live situations · 100 runs per variant · 229 simulated traders · prices indexed to 100

Bottom line
  • Election rally: how the rally behaves after the first week tells you what kind it is. If it reflects a real reassessment of value, the model keeps roughly half of it after a month (median +0.8%). If it is only mood, the gain is gone within a month (median -0.5%, below the starting price in 58% of runs).
  • Fed, 9 Dec: the most likely outcome barely moves the market (5-day median -0.3%). The risk is a surprise 50bp hike. Weighting all three outcomes by their odds gives a median of -0.3%, a 22% chance of a drop of more than 2%, and a 1-in-20 worst case of -5.5%.
  • Energy: liquidity, not the news itself, decides the damage. If market makers pull back, the first-day drop goes from -1.2% to -5.9%, the bid–ask spread goes from 64 to 315 bps, and the 1-in-20 worst drop deepens to -27.8%.

What is happening today

  • Democrats won control of Congress in the 3 Nov midterms. The S&P 500 rose about 1.5% intraday and the Dow 2.4–2.9%, its best session in months.
  • Oil extended its slide, as the result was read as opposition to the Iran war. Big tech stocks bounced about 3% after a selloff.
  • The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% on 28 Oct, with two officials voting for a hike. Markets price about a 77% chance of a 25bp hike on 9 Dec.

3 · Energy stocks while oil slides

A typical energy stock has its value cut 5% on lower oil, mood turns negative and volatility is higher (1.5%/day). It is run twice: once with normal liquidity, and once where market makers step back for 5 days (stress 0.6), as dealers often do in fast-moving commodity markets.

The value cut is the same 5% in both runs, but thin liquidity brings the drop forward to day one, makes trading about 5× more expensive and makes the 1-in-20 worst case much deeper (-16.6% → -27.8%). Assumptions and limits

  • No real price history was used. This environment's network rules block market-data sites (FRED, Yahoo, Stooq), so prices are on a 100-point index and the TimesFM baseline was skipped. To add it, allow a data host or provide CSVs and rerun.
  • Fair-value volatility is 0.8%/day for the index and 1.5%/day for energy. Each trading day is 10 simulation steps.
  • Fed outcome sizes: as-expected hike -0.5% fair value; hold +2.5%; 50bp hike -4.5%. Odds: 75% / 20% / 5%. The market prices the hike at 77%; the 5% for a 50bp hike is my assumption.
  • Trader mix: bundled baseline population: 4 market makers, 5 institutions, 30 value investors, 25 trend followers, 15 mean reverters, 150 retail traders.
  • This is a stylised model of how shocks spread through different kinds of traders. It is not calibrated to the S&P 500 or any stock, not a price forecast, and not investment advice.
