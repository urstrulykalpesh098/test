Monday is a waiting day before Tuesday's CPI (8:30am) and CoreWeave's results (after the close). The likeliest shape is a flat open, a chip-led attempt to bounce off Asia's new support measures, and a drift back as nobody adds risk before CPI. Hold that shape loosely. It rests on one anecdotal source. VIX at 25.6 says a 2% day either way is ordinary.

The one number to check before the bell. The December S&P future around 9:00 ET, minus about 22 points of carry (futures normally trade above the index because of interest rates net of dividends: 3.9% − 1.2% over 40 days). Friday's 7,405 future was really the index flat at about 7,383. If that adjusted price is below 7,325, Monday opens under the 200-day and the call moves to 7,290. Above 7,420, the selling is washing out and the call moves to 7,430. Sunday-night oil, Korea and Taiwan feed into it, so watch those first.

The read across the channels. The pros say buy while retail, the news tone and crypto all say fear. That split is the classic setup for a contrarian bounce. Two things are missing for it: retail has not capitulated (it is still buying 3× ETFs), and nothing that caused the drop has been resolved. Fear without capitulation usually means chop, not a turn. The turn needs a catalyst, and the first candidate arrives Tuesday at 8:30.

Three ways the open can land

If adjusted futures < 7,325
Close ≈ 7,290

The open is already under the 200-day. A close below it becomes more likely than not (above 55%), and trend followers' selling starts lining up for Tuesday. Most likely cause: an oil spike or a failed Korean session.

If 7,325 to 7,420
Close ≈ 7,370

The base call. The market chops and waits for CPI, with a range of about 2% and a slight fade. This is where most of the probability sits.

If adjusted futures > 7,420
Close ≈ 7,430

The selling has washed out and short covering takes over. Vol-control funds have already cut, and AAII bears are at 48%. Odds of an up day rise to about 60%.

Risks this call underweights

  • An overnight gap from Hormuz, in either direction. Brent's −12% week priced in de-escalation. A mine or a strike that reverses that can open the S&P down 1.5%. A deal can do the opposite.
  • Korea feeding into US leveraged ETFs. The margin waiver skips foreign-share collateral. A weak KOSPI means forced selling of SOXL and TQQQ during US hours, and the close-of-day rebalance amplifies it.
  • A washed-out short squeeze. Investors cut the most risk since 2020, AAII bears were at 48% and Fear & Greed hit 20. A +1.5–2% day is not a tail here. It sits inside the 80% range, at its upper end.
