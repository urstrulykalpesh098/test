Ten years of free data say the desk's ranges were sized for a storm, and no simple signal beats the plain up-rate on direction.

What the self-research loop decided

Each belief is checked on data before 2022 and again on 2022 onwards, which it was never fitted to. These are proposals only. Rules change only when Rk says yes.

1.6x ranges catch 98% of days, not 80%

The curve shows how often a one-day S&P range would have held at each width, as a multiple of the VIX-implied move, over every day since late 2016. The orange line is the 80% target. Since the long-run VIX overprices the next day, a range of about 0.75x already holds 80%.

November 2026 was the exception: in the last 15 days 1.6x held 87%. The adaptive band, built from half VIX and half recent realized moves, held 77% out of sample, but only 53% in this shock. The proposal is to use the wider of the two while VIX is above 25.

Multi-asset board: ten markets, graded automatically

Each range is sized from that market's recent moves, with a width fitted on the first half of its history. "Held" shows how often that width caught the next value on the second half. The up chance is the market's long-run up-rate: an honest baseline to beat, not a view.

Seven specialists, no edge on direction archived

Each analyst watches one kind of evidence, gets refit every quarter on past data only, and is scored on about 1,950 days it never saw. A lower Brier score is better, and 0.25 is a coin flip. The plain up-rate is the line to beat.

The best of them beat the up-rate by 0.0003, which is noise. The useful finding is that the desk shouldn't expect daily direction calls to carry much skill. Its edge, if it has one, is in sizing ranges and in reading events.

Archived on 27 Nov 2026. They no longer run each night or feed any call. Their code and these scores are kept in the repo, so any of them can be switched back on.
